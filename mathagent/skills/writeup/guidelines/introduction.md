# Style guidelines: introduction of a paper

- I1 [required] Motivation. The first paragraphs say what the problem is and why it matters, before any technical statement.
- I2 [required] Literature. What is already known is described with citations. Every statement about earlier work has a
  citation from the bibliography records; nothing is attributed from memory.
- I3 [required] Positioning. The text says what is new compared with the cited work: which gap the paper fills.
- I4 [required] Main result. The main result is stated precisely in the introduction, or informally with a reference to
  the precise statement.
- I5 [required] Method. A short paragraph gives the idea of the proof and what is new in it.
- I6 [required] Organisation. The introduction ends with the plan of the paper, section by section, with `\ref`.
- I7 [style] Restraint. No proof in the introduction, and only the notation needed to state the results.
